  • So… what are Charaktêres (singular: charaktêr)? Well, they’re a thing in Graeco-egyptian, jewish, and arabic magic that are letter-shaped signs lacking both sematic and phonetic correlations[1]. In her 2013 thesis Kirsten Dzwiza studied 94 magical texts and recorded 699 different charaktêres occurring over 943 times.[2] The character forms are mostly nonsensical and may include ring-letters, balls, points, closed elements, separate strokes, linear elements, small element, and hieroglyphs.[2] The most common forms consist of asterisks and configurations of straight lines with small circles at their ends.[3] The signs appear mostly on apotropaic spells and phylacteries, but also on a few ancient curse tablets.[1] NOW… how to use them? A guide to making charaktêres!

  • Draw a shape, here, we’ll take two intersecting lines.
    • Now normally, in making a charaktêr, what we do is put a circle on the end point (or the terminal points). The circle should not be big, it should be small (like a node).
  • If two lines intersect, what we do is put a node on the intersection.
  • If two lines touch to form a sharp corner, put a node there.
  • Basically
    • Cap the loose ends/Terminal points
    • Cap the sharp points
    • Cap the intersections
    • That’s it

Things to note

  • Do not try to translate Charaktêres, they are supposed to be untranslatable.
  • Do not try to make charaktêres have meaning (like some charaktêr meaning A or B). They are supposed to be untranslatable.
  • Do not make the charaktêres have a script (unless…?).
